0 · How this guide works

Every purchase sits behind either no gate (safe to buy any time), an anchor (long lead time, order first), or a gate (a physical measurement or dry-fit that must happen before money is spent). The order is arranged so that the parts most likely to be wrong are bought last, after the parts they depend on are on the bench.

The four things that are not locked and will stay unlocked until hardware is measured: exact spoke lengths, main fuse type and rating, tire pressures, and chainline / 32T clearance after the motor is installed. Everything else in the BOM is mature enough to order in its phase.

Checkboxes save in this browser only (key prefix krampus_). This page is the working BOM for the Krampus; the Kilo TT build bible is unchanged.

Carted lengths (rear DS 286, front-disc + rear NDS 288, front right 290, all 3-cross) are PROVISIONAL. They came from approximate hub dimensions. On a 36h 3-cross wheel, 2 mm is the difference between full thread engagement and a spoke that bottoms.

Sanity check: extend the 73° seat-tube line up to stack height and its X is about −190, so 470 − (−190) ≈ 660 mm, which matches the published effective top tube. The geometry is internally consistent.

Keep-out and usable cavity

  • Until the tubes are measured (Gate A), design against a 25–30 mm keep-out band from each centerline. That covers tube radius, bag wall, isolation foam, and installation clearance.
  • Grow the keep-out substantially at the BB junction and the head-tube junction: gussets and weld beads eat space there, and the CYC motor's controller housing sits right at the BB.
  • Expect the seat-tube OD to be around 35 mm given the 33.1 mm clamp spec. Measure it anyway.
  • Bottle bosses on the down tube and seat tube are your tray anchor points if you don't want to strap. One standard + one three-pack on this frame.

Cell arrangement

Lay the 21700s sideways across the bike, 70 mm length left-to-right. In side view each cell is then a ~21 mm circle and the pack can follow the triangle in a staggered (honeycomb) array with a row pitch of about 21 × sin 60° ≈ 18.2 mm before holders. Allow ~22 mm pitch with holders and fish paper.

  Example 80-cell honeycomb footprint (rows 8–14, staggered), cells sideways:

     O O O O O O O O O O O O O O    14      trim/extend rows to reach exactly 80

  Approx. envelope at 22 mm pitch: ~310 mm wide × ~135 mm tall × ~75 mm deep (70 mm cell + nickel)
  A plain 20 × 4 rectangle would be ~440 × 88 × 75 mm. Both fit an XL triangle easily.
  • Cells low and forward along the down tube; BMS on the outer face of the tray in free air, not buried in the cell block. The Daly's fan is only useful in daylight; inside the bag it's a lint pump.
  • Series path snakes through 20 groups of 4. Plan the nickel so every series link is a doubled 0.2 × 30 mm strip or a nickel-copper sandwich; 120 A peak battery current is not a single-strip job.
  • Bag is weather protection only. Rigid tray carries the load, with a removable BMS panel for service.
Next design step after Gate A: with the five measurements (two tube diameters, seat-tube OD, inside width, down-tube length), draw the cavity at 1:1, place all 80 cells, BMS, fuse, XT90-S, and wire channels, and cut a cardboard tray. Only when the cardboard tray sits in the frame with the motor installed do the cells get ordered.

8 · Phase 5 — battery, in order tray before cells

84 V full charge. This pack is above the 60 V threshold where a hand-to-hand path is a real shock hazard, and 4P of P50B can deliver fault current into the hundreds of amps. Every rule from the Kilo TT bible applies with less margin: one-hand rule, DMM before every mate, insulated tools, and no live work on the series stack.

9 · Phase 6 — assembly and commissioning

  1. Headset, fork, stem, bar. Leave 20–30 mm of spacers above the stem until the fit is settled.
  2. Wheels on, tires seated tubeless, sealant in. Starting pressures at ~300 lb system: PROVISIONAL rear 28 psi, front 22 psi. Come down 2 psi at a time until compliance arrives without rim strikes, squirm, or burping. The rear carries most of the load and will want more than the front.
  3. Brakes: adapters, 203 rotors, calipers, hoses cut after the cockpit dry-fit, bleed, bed-in on the stand.
  4. Motor (already fitted at Gate C), cassette with spacers set for chainline, derailleur, chain, shifter cable.
  5. Display, throttle, brake sensors, harness routed clear of the rotors and the crank.
  6. Tray + pack in, XT90-S mated with the fuse in circuit. First power-on with the rear wheel off the ground: app connect, throttle response, brake cutoff verified before any ride.
  7. First rides at reduced battery current in the CYC app, then step up. Re-tension wheels after 50 km.

12 · "Can I buy this today?"

Is it the frameset, the rims, or the CYC kit?
  └─ YES → buy now (Phase 1). Put the SW102 voltage in the CYC order note.
  └─ NO ↓
Has the frame arrived and Gate A been done?
  └─ NO  → wait. (Exception: Terminal bar if stock is the risk.)
  └─ YES ↓
Is it hubs, tires, cassette, derailleur, shifter, brakes, rotors, cockpit, saddle, pedals?
  └─ YES → buy now (Phases 2–4). Headset/star nut only if not in the box.
  └─ NO ↓
Is it spokes or nipples?
  └─ Hubs on the bench and lengths recomputed?  NO → wait.  YES → buy.
Is it the chain?
  └─ Motor installed, 32T cleared, chainline set?  NO → wait.  YES → buy.
Is it the BMS, fuse, wiring, charger?
  └─ Cardboard tray fits with the motor in?  NO → wait.  YES → buy (BMS first).
Is it the cells?
  └─ BMS in hand, tray drawn around it, fuse chosen?  NO → wait.  YES → last purchase.
