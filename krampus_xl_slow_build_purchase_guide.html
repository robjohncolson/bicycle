1. The governing rules

Buy foundations first

Frame, motor, rims and hubs establish nearly every dimension that matters later.

Measure before dependent parts

Spokes, battery tray, chainline corrections, fuse/wire layout, and possibly headset parts should wait for physical verification.

Do not “complete a cart” for its own sake

Free shipping is not a reason to buy an uncertain part. A $12 shipping charge is cheaper than one wrong $150 component.

Use return windows as insurance, not process

Prefer purchases whose interfaces are already known. Do not rely on “I can always return it.”

Important correction to the earlier notes: the current Surly frameset page explicitly lists the 33.1 mm seatpost clamp and both thru-axles as included, but it does not list a headset as an included component. It lists the required headset standards (ZS44/28.6 upper, EC44/30 lower). Therefore: do not assume a headset is in the frame box. Receive the frame first, inventory the box, then buy any missing headset/star-nut/top-cap parts.

2. Purchase order — deliberately piecemeal

1

Foundation: secure the hard-to-substitute chassis

Buy: Surly Krampus XL frameset. This establishes all real clearances, fork/steerer hardware, brake mounts, BB shell, rear spacing and the physical battery triangle.

2

Motor before drivetrain fine-tuning or battery fabrication

Buy: CYC X1 Pro Gen 4 configured 32T/53T, SW102, 68/73/83 mm, thumb throttle, 160 mm cranks, brake sensors. The Krampus BB is 73 mm.

Do not yet fabricate: battery tray. Do not yet solve: final chainring offset. Install/mount the motor first and inspect 32T stay clearance and chainline.

3

Wheel hardware: rims and hubs first; spokes second

Buy: two 29" × 36h Velocity Dually rims, Jones HD/e 36h rear, DT Swiss 350 Hybrid 36h front. These are low-risk because their interfaces are fixed by the frame.

Hold spokes until the actual hubs and rims can be measured. The 286/288/290 mm Alpine III plan is a good provisional calculation, not a purchase trigger.

4

Wheel consumables and tires

Only after the wheel dimensions are locked: order Alpine III spokes in the measured lengths, DT brass Pro Head Pro Lock nipples, Dirt Wizard 29×3.0 tires, Velotape, valves and sealant. Build 3-cross unless a final spoke calculator or hub maker specifically indicates otherwise.

5

Drivetrain and brakes

The CYC 15–30 steel cassette, Advent super-short clutch derailleur, single-click shifter and KMC e9 chain are a matched concept. The brakes are also mostly independent once frame/fork IS mounts are confirmed.

Dry-fit the right-hand controls before hose cutting: MT5 lever + thumb throttle + Advent shifter + brake sensor need to coexist on a 34° swept bar.

6

Cockpit and contact points

Terminal bar, 50 mm Thomson X4, 30.9 × 410 Thomson post, WTB Volt 142, GA2 Fat and OneUp pedals are low-risk after the frame is physically present. Do a dry assembly before trimming any steerer or brake hose.

7

Battery only after the bike becomes a physical mock-up

Install motor, wheels and crank/chainring first. Put the frame on the ground. Then make a full-size cardboard/foam-board battery template inside the actual triangle. Only after that should the tray geometry, BMS position, cell pattern and removable-pack handle/connector be frozen.

This is the highest-value delay in the entire project. Eighty cells will easily fit in the XL Krampus triangle, so there is no reason to commit early to a poor rectangle. Optimize service access, BMS cooling, wiring and removal instead.
8

Battery electrical parts after the mechanical tray is proven

Then buy cells, BMS, charger, fuse, wire and connectors. Build the battery as a rigid removable assembly; the frame bag should contain/protect the pack, not be its structure.

9

Ride, then optimize

Rigid fork, strap-on lights and current luggage first. Suspension fork, Supernova lighting, alternate commuter tires and cosmetic refinements happen only after real commuting exposes a need.

4. Measurement gates — the “no returns” checklist

Frame arrival gate

  • Photograph and record serial/condition before assembly.
  • Measure BB shell width: expected 73 mm threaded.
  • Confirm front axle 15×110 and rear axle hardware for 12×148.
  • Confirm seatpost clamp is included (Surly lists 33.1 mm included).
  • Inventory headset-related hardware; do not infer “headset standard” means “headset included.”

Motor fit gate

  • Mount X1 to the actual BB.
  • Check 32T chainring-to-chainstay clearance.
  • Record chainring centerline relative to frame center.
  • With rear wheel/hub present, record cassette center and useful cog chainlines.
  • Do not correct chainline until both motor and final rear hub/cassette are installed.

Wheel gate

  • Measure Dually ERD on the actual pair even though published ERD is 604 mm.
  • Measure DT and Jones flange diameters.
  • Measure left/right center-to-flange distance.
  • Then run a spoke calculator for 36h, 3-cross, each wheel side independently.
  • Only then order Alpine III lengths.

Battery gate

  • Bike assembled enough to stand at ride height.
  • Motor and all cables occupy their real space.
  • Full-size removable mock-up passes through the frame opening.
  • Mock-up can be removed without dropping motor or wheel.
  • BMS has exposed cooling path and service access.
  • Main connector cannot be accidentally shorted during daily removal.

5. Wheel system

The goal is durability rather than low rotational mass: 36 spokes front and rear, wide 29+ rims, brass nipples, a heavy-duty rear freehub, and an e-bike-oriented front hub.

Published rim data

Velocity Dually 29: 45 mm external, 39 mm internal, 18 mm tall, 675 g, ERD 604 mm, tubeless/clincher, 622 BSD, Presta 32–40 mm.

Jones rear data

36h, 12×148 Boost, 6-bolt, available steel HG + steel axle; Jones specifically recommends that combination for maximum stiffness/durability. Published spoke-calculation dimensions: 62 mm flange diameter, 37 mm left / 22 mm right from center.

Spoke rule

Keep DT Alpine III + brass Pro Head Pro Lock as the material choice, but treat lengths as unresolved until the exact DT front dimensions and actual rim ERD are measured.

Why this avoids a costly mistake: buying the rims and hubs first cannot make the spoke order obsolete. Buying spokes first can.

6. Motor, chainline and drivetrain

The Krampus uses a 73 mm threaded BB. Surly publishes a conservative single-chainring clearance of 30T Boost; the planned CYC uses a different motor/spider arrangement, so the published bicycle-crank limit does not directly answer whether the CYC 32T clears. Physical mock-up wins.

  1. Mount motor and 32T before assuming anything.
  2. Install rear hub + 15–30 cassette.
  3. Sight/measure chainline across all five cogs.
  4. If 32T physically touches the stay, solve with an appropriate smaller compatible ring/offset solution rather than changing frame reflexively.
  5. Set derailleur high/low stops for five usable cogs even though the Advent indexing family is 9-speed spacing.

7. Battery plan — postpone the irreversible decisions

Target: 20s4p P50B = 80 cells, 84 V full charge, nominal 72-ish V class, 20 Ah. The XL Krampus triangle has ample area for 80 cells; the design problem is not “will it fit?” but “how do we make it safe, removable, cool and serviceable?”

Mechanical architecture

  • Cells transverse to the bike are likely the most area-efficient side-profile arrangement.
  • Use a rigid tray/cassette; do not let the fabric bag be structural.
  • Design the pack to come out daily without unplugging a fragile long balance harness.
  • Place the fan-cooled Daly on an exposed face, not buried inside insulation.
  • Keep high-current connection, charging connection and service/balance access physically organized and strain-relieved.

Battery safety: an 80-cell 20S pack can deliver destructive fault current. Design the enclosure, insulation, strain relief, fuse and conductor routing as a system. Do not treat the BMS as the only short-circuit safety layer.

8. Brakes

The current Surly Salmon Skin Silver page lists 51 mm I.S. mounts front and rear with 203 mm maximum rotors. Magura’s current MT manual identifies QM5 as IS→203 front and QM9 as IS→203 rear. That supports the current MT5 + MDR-P 203 ×2 + QM5/QM9 plan.

  • Keep hoses uncut until bar, stem and controls are dry-fitted at final height/rotation.
  • Confirm the CYC brake sensors supplied are appropriate for the hydraulic MT5 lever setup.
  • Buy a bleed kit and spare pads later; they are maintenance items, not blockers.

13. “Can I buy this today?” decision tree

Does the part depend on a dimension from a part I do not physically own yet?
 ├─ YES → HOLD.
 │        Buy the upstream part first, measure, then order.
 └─ NO
    ├─ Does the exact SKU/interface have more than one option?
    │   ├─ YES → VERIFY the cart line before paying.
    │   └─ NO
    └─ Would waiting materially risk losing a scarce/long-lead item?
        ├─ YES → BUY if compatibility is already fixed.
        └─ NO  → There is no penalty for waiting.

Examples: frame = buy; rims = buy; hubs = buy after checking exact axle/drilling/driver; spokes = hold; cells = hold; seatpost = safe; headset = hold until frame-box inventory; suspension fork = much later.

This guide is intentionally conservative. Its purpose is not to finish the bike quickly; it is to turn each purchase into the consequence of a verified prior decision.
